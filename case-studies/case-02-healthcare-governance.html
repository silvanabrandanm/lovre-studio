01 — Context

The product worked. Almost nobody used it the way it was meant to be used.

Incident reports were taking 18 minutes to fill out — on a ward, between patients, on a shared workstation. So clinicians stopped reporting near-misses. Policy attestations went into mass-click mode at the end of every quarter. The risk team had data, but the data wasn't trustworthy, and they knew it.

The brief wasn't "make the forms prettier." It was: design a governance product that earns its place on a clinician's day, and gives the risk team something they can defend in front of an inspector.

02 — Approach

Three principles, ruthlessly applied.

01

Designed for the 90-second window

Every interaction had to survive being interrupted. Resumable drafts, local persistence, single-tap entry points from any context. We measured every flow against time-on-task, not screen count.

02

Tiered the form to the risk

A near-miss should not require the same form as a serious incident. We worked with the risk team to define three tiers, each with a different level of evidence and follow-up — and let the system route them.

03

Made the audit trail a first-class output

Every action — view, edit, escalate, sign-off — became a typed event with a timestamp and an actor. The audit view was designed alongside the entry view, not bolted on at the end.

04

Piloted in two trusts before the full rollout

One acute, one community. Different cultures, different reporting patterns. The design system absorbed the differences as configuration, not forks.

"We stopped having to chase clinicians for reports. The product asks the right amount, at the right moment, and the data we get back is actually usable."

03 — Outcomes

What changed for the people doing the work.

  • Form abandonment dropped 54% across both pilot trusts Sustained at 12-month follow-up.
  • Near-miss reporting volume tripled The category that the old form effectively suppressed.
  • Quarterly attestation completed inside SLA at 94% of sites Up from 61% the previous year, on the same staff base.
  • Audit trail accepted in two CQC inspections without follow-up Used as a positive reference in one of the inspection reports.
